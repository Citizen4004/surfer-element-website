## The short version

Taghazout's famous point breaks (Anchor Point, Killer Point, Boilers) are for experienced surfers. Beginners on this coast learn at the sandy beach breaks just south, around Tamraght. Our three houses are in Tamraght, about 6 to 8 km south of Taghazout, roughly 10 minutes by car. Surfer Element is paid by these three houses and only represents them.

This page covers the points Taghazout is known for, plainly: what kind of wave each one is, who it suits, when it works and how busy it gets. Most people who come here on a surf week spend it on the beach breaks, and that's fine. You might paddle out at Anchor on a later trip, but not on day three.

## Is Taghazout good for beginner surfers?

Yes as a base for a surf trip, no for the famous points. Anchor Point, Killer Point, Boilers and Mysteries break over rock, often with a rock entry and a busy line-up, and they suit experienced surfers. Within a short drive, though, there are sandy beach breaks where beginners learn, and which one works on a given day depends on the swell, tide and wind.

The confusion usually comes from photos. Taghazout's reputation was built on long right-hand points that peel down rocky headlands when a big north-west swell arrives in winter. Those are the waves in the photos, and none of them is where you'll stand up for the first time.

## Can beginners surf Anchor Point?

No. Anchor Point is a long right-hand point break over rock and sand. Getting in usually means a timed jump off slippery rocks between sets, and [Surfnerd](https://surfnerd.com/surf-forecast/anchor-point) calls the crowd the main hazard, especially on smaller days. [mondo.surf](https://www.mondo.surf/surf-spot/anchor-point/guide/8559) rates it intermediate and says it is not a spot to learn on, and [WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/anchor_point/) lists it for experienced surfers.

How we'd plan your way there:

1. Your first week or two: sandy beach breaks with a coach, where you learn to paddle, pop up and ride white water, then unbroken waves. Around here, that means the breaks near Tamraght.
2. Once you can catch and ride unbroken waves on your own and turn along them: a mellower wave on a small day, ideally with someone who knows it. Ask us before you book whether the coaching at your house covers that step.
3. Later, on another trip: Anchor, on a day that suits your level, around low to mid tide, after you've watched how the locals get in and out over the rocks.

If you're still deciding whether a surf week is for you at all, our learning-to-surf guide covers that side of it.

## The famous points, and who they're really for

Spot levels below come from neutral surf databases and guides (surf-forecast.com, WannaSurf, mondo.surf, Surfnerd and The Surf Atlas), checked on 6 November 2026. They often disagree. Where they do, we go with the more cautious rating, because a rock-bottomed point is the wrong place to find out you weren't ready.

### Anchor Point

Who it's for: intermediate to advanced surfers. Not beginners.

A long right-hand point about a kilometre north of Taghazout village, also written "Anka Point" ([WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/anchor_point/)). It needs a north-west groundswell and works best in north-east offshore wind around low tide, according to [surf-forecast.com](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/La-Pointedes-Ancres), which also warns about rocks in the line-up; mondo.surf says all tides work, with low to mid the most popular. WannaSurf has it starting at about 1 to 1.5 m and holding swells of 5 m and more, with rides of 300 to 500 m on a good day. Reports on crowds differ: surf-forecast.com calls it a popular wave that can get crowded and Surfnerd says the line-up is busy, while WannaSurf and mondo.surf describe it as often empty. Summer is mostly flat here.

### Killer Point

Who it's for: advanced surfers; some sources say strong intermediates. Not beginners.

A right-hand point further north along the coast from Anchor, about 1.4 km from it according to [Surfnerd](https://surfnerd.com/surf-forecast/killer-point), breaking over flat rocks, with very long rides on a good day ([WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/killer_point/index.html)). It wants a bigger north-west swell: WannaSurf has it starting at about 2 m. The sources disagree on tide: WannaSurf says low tide on the falling tide, while [surf-forecast.com](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Killer-Point) says it's surfable at all tides. WannaSurf lists rocks and localism as hazards, and surf-forecast lists rocks, locals and sharks. On level, [The Surf Atlas](https://thesurfatlas.com/surfing-morocco/taghazout-surf/) rates it for experts and says the paddle out alone is hard work, Surfnerd says intermediate and up, surf-forecast users rate its difficulty 3.5 out of 5, and WannaSurf lists it for "all surfers". We don't agree with that last one for anyone in their first weeks.

### Boilers

Who it's for: experienced surfers only.

A heavy right-hand reef break over boulders, north of Taghazout on the coast road towards Essaouira ([WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/boilers/index.html)). The Surf Atlas calls it the farthest north of the main Taghazout breaks and notes a fast take-off close to a rusting shipwreck. WannaSurf has it starting at about 1.5 to 2 m of swell, best at low tide, and its notes and comments warn of a difficult entry and exit when it's big and holes in the rock at the entry. [Surf-forecast.com](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Boilers) lists rips and rocks and says it gets busy when it's on. The Surf Atlas rates it intermediate and up; we stay with WannaSurf's "experienced surfers".

### Mysteries

Who it's for: upper-intermediate surfers and up. Not beginners.

A short right-hander just north of Taghazout, between Anchor and Killer Point, with a tight, ledgy take-off over rock ([WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/mystery_point/index.html), listed there as "Mystery Point", also called Mysteries). The Surf Atlas rates it for upper intermediates, and experts only when it's big. It works from about 1 to 1.5 m of north-west swell. The best tide is disputed: WannaSurf says low to mid, while [surf-forecast.com](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Mystery-Point) says around high tide. It's crowded when it works, and WannaSurf mentions localism and broken glass on the cliff path down.

### Hash Point

Who it's for: improving to intermediate surfers who can already ride unbroken waves and handle a rocky entry.

A fast right-hand point in Taghazout village itself. On a good day it can run through to the fishermen's beach ([WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/hash_point/index.html)). Rides are shorter than at Anchor, and WannaSurf says the rock entry can be tricky, especially at low tide. [Surf-forecast.com](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Hash-Point) gives low tide as best, north-west swell and north-east offshore wind, and warns about rocks. The databases are more generous than we are here: WannaSurf and The Surf Atlas list it for all levels, and surf-forecast.com says some waves may suit beginners at times. The Surf Atlas sees it as the step up for improvers leaving the beginner beaches. We agree, but we'd still want you past the white-water stage first.

### Panorama

Who it's for: intermediates. Sources are split on whether improving beginners can surf it on small days.

The break at the south end of Taghazout village, in front of the Panorama restaurant, with sand and rock ([WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/panorama/index.html)). WannaSurf describes it as a right point that starts working below 1 m and is best at low to mid tide. It likes a bit of north in the swell, and a more westerly swell makes it fast and hard to ride. [Surf-forecast.com](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Panoramas) calls it a beach and reef break with lefts and rights, lists rips and rocks as hazards, says summer is mostly flat, and says small days may suit beginners at times. The Surf Atlas, on the other hand, rates it intermediate and up and says it can be steep close to the cliffs. It's the gentlest of Taghazout's well-known names, but it's not a first-week wave.

## Where do beginners surf near Taghazout?

Mostly at the sandy beach breaks just south of Taghazout, around Tamraght. The spot databases rate Crocro and Devil's Rock as suitable for beginners on smaller days, with Banana Beach at the southern end towards Aourir (Banana Village) as a small-day option. The main Tamraght beach between them is rated intermediate. Which beach works on a given morning depends on the swell, tide and wind.

Banana Point, the right-hand point beside Banana Beach, is where the sources split. mondo.surf and surf-forecast.com list it as an intermediate wave with rocks, rips and crowds. [The Surf Atlas's Taghazout guide](https://thesurfatlas.com/surfing-morocco/taghazout-surf/) rates it beginner to intermediate and calls it a "beginner mecca", but also says the line-up is almost always packed, and its [Tamraght guide](https://thesurfatlas.com/surfing-morocco/tamraght-surf/) puts the take-off right under the rocks. We side with the cautious rating: not in your first week.

## When is the surf season in Taghazout?

Roughly October to April, when north-west Atlantic swells arrive most often, with the biggest swells usually from December to March; beginners can also learn in the smaller summer waves ([The Surf Atlas, Morocco surf season](https://thesurfatlas.com/surf-travel/morocco-surf-season/)). The points need that winter swell. Surf-forecast.com describes summer at [Anchor](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/La-Pointedes-Ancres) and [Panorama](https://www.surf-forecast.com/breaks/Panoramas) as mostly flat. Month-level detail (swell, water temperature, wetsuit, crowds) is in our [month-by-month guide to surfing Tamraght in winter](/guide/surfing-tamraght-by-month/), and it applies to Taghazout's points too.

## How far is Tamraght from Taghazout's surf spots?

Tamraght is about 6 to 8 km south of Taghazout, roughly 10 minutes by car (from [OpenStreetMap routing](https://www.openstreetmap.org/directions) and [Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Tamraght/Taghazout), checked 2026-11-06). Panorama and Hash Point are in Taghazout village, Anchor Point is about a kilometre north of it ([WannaSurf](https://www.wannasurf.com/spot/Africa/Morocco/South/anchor_point/)), Mysteries and Killer Point are further along the same stretch, and Boilers is the furthest, on the coast road towards Essaouira. Taxis run along the coast road between the villages. Local buses 32 and 33 run from central Agadir up the coast road through Tamraght to Taghazout ([Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Aourir)). Fares are a few dirhams, paid in cash on board. Ask locally for the current stop and times.

If you're flying in, our guide to [getting from Agadir airport to Tamraght](/guide/agadir-airport-to-tamraght/) covers the drive, and the transfer is included both ways when you book a house through us.

## Crowds and etiquette at the points

The points are where crowding and localism show up. Anchor and Mysteries get busy when they're working, WannaSurf lists localism at Killer and Mysteries, and surf-forecast.com lists locals at Killer. The basics:

- The surfer closest to the peak has priority, and if someone is already up and riding a wave, don't paddle for it ([SurfGirl Magazine](https://surfgirlmag.com/etiquette-right-of-way-and-drop-ins/)).
- No drop-ins, no solo sessions, always a leash. That's the [Government of Jersey's](https://gov.je/stayingsafe/outdoor/pages/beach.aspx) beach-safety advice, and it applies here too.
- Watch the entry first. Surfnerd describes getting in at Anchor as a timed jump off slippery rocks between sets, and WannaSurf calls the rock entry at Hash tricky, especially at low tide. Watch where and when the regulars get in and out before you try it.
- Stay in smaller surf until you have the skills for bigger waves, as [Wikipedia's surfing article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing) advises. Our own rule of thumb: if you have to ask whether you're ready for Anchor, you aren't yet.
