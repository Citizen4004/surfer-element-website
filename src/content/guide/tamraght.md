> Tamraght is a small surf village on Morocco's Atlantic coast, between Agadir and Taghazout, in the Agadir-Ida-Ou-Tanane prefecture (Souss-Massa region). It is a separate village from Taghazout, about 6 to 8 km to the south. Beginners come here for the gentler breaks close to the village.

*Published by Surfer Element, a surf-camp booking agency based in Tamraght. We work with three houses in the village, and they pay us.*

## Where is Tamraght?

Tamraght is about 16 to 18 km north of central Agadir, roughly 20 minutes by car, and about 6 to 8 km south of Taghazout, in the Agadir-Ida-Ou-Tanane prefecture of the Souss-Massa region. The drive to Taghazout is roughly 10 minutes by car. (Road figures from OpenStreetMap routing and Rome2Rio, checked 2026-11-06; exact distances vary by a kilometre or two depending on where you start and stop. Administrative location per [OpenStreetMap/Nominatim](https://nominatim.openstreetmap.org/search?q=Tamraght+Morocco&format=json&addressdetails=1).)

Agadir Al Massira Airport (AGA) is the usual way in. Tamraght is about 40 km from the airport, roughly 40 to 50 minutes by road depending on traffic. The airport section below covers the options.

On maps and booking sites the village is also listed under other spellings and neighbouring place names, such as "Tamraght, Aourir" (Aourir is the nearby town it is administratively part of, according to OpenStreetMap). If you're searching listings, try more than one spelling.

## Is Tamraght the same as Taghazout?

No. Tamraght and Taghazout are two separate villages on the same coast road, about 6 to 8 km apart. Some booking sites list Tamraght surf camps under "Taghazout", which is where the mix-up comes from. All three houses we work with are in Tamraght.

Taghazout is the better-known name and has its own famous point breaks, which the surf section below points you to. Staying near Taghazout is a topic of its own, and we haven't published that guide yet.

## Is Tamraght good for beginner surfers?

Yes, on the right days: the beach breaks near the village suit beginners when the swell is small, and winter swell makes some days too big. [The Surf Atlas](https://thesurfatlas.com/surf-travel/morocco-surf-season/) describes mellow beaches near Tamraght as suited to beginners, improvers and longboarders, particularly in the smaller summer waves, and [Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Aourir) calls Tamraght a neighbouring village of Taghazout with beginner-friendly beach breaks. Winter swell is bigger, so the right spot depends on the day's swell and tide.

[Tamraght's surf spots by level and tide](/guide/surfing-tamraght/) covers each break, when it works and what to watch for. No single spot is the beginner spot; it changes with the day.

When's the surf season? Roughly October to April, when north-west Atlantic swells arrive most often, with the biggest swells usually from December to March; beginners can also learn in the smaller summer waves. [Surfing Tamraght month by month](/guide/surfing-tamraght-by-month/) breaks the winter down.

Taghazout's famous point breaks are a later step, not week one. If you want to see what you're building towards, read about [Taghazout's famous point breaks](/guide/surfing-taghazout/). A guide to learning to surf in Morocco is also on our list.

## What's the weather like in Tamraght?

Tamraght has a mild Atlantic coastal climate: days are generally warm and dry for much of the year, mornings and evenings are cooler, and the ocean stays cool even when the air is hot. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadir) classifies nearby Agadir as semi-arid (Köppen BSh) with warm summers and mild winters, and [Weather2Travel](https://www.weather2travel.com/morocco/agadir/climate) calls it a hot semi-arid climate. ## What's Tamraght like day to day?

Tamraght is a working Moroccan village with a lot of surfers in it, and it has no resort infrastructure.

- Pace: [Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Aourir) describes Tamraght as quieter than Taghazout. Expect a village rather than nightlife; ask your house what evenings look like.
- Cash and cards: at the three houses, you pay the balance of your stay in cash to the house when you arrive, and the 15% deposit goes to us when you book. Wikivoyage warns that card payment is not common in the village and points to Agadir for banks and larger shopping, so plan to carry cash for extras.

Tamraght suits you if you want to surf, eat well and keep evenings quiet. If you're after nightlife or resort facilities, Agadir is the nearer big town.

## What is there to do in Tamraght besides surfing?

Mostly day trips. Tamraght itself is quiet, so non-surf plans mean excursions to Paradise Valley, the Souk El Had market in Agadir and an argan-oil village. Souk El Had is a large walled market that [Wikivoyage's Agadir guide](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Agadir) lists as open Tuesday to Sunday, selling everything from souvenirs and leather goods to fruit and vegetables. Ask your house about timings and distances for each excursion. At all three houses we work with, these excursions are included from 5 nights.

Daily yoga is also in the standard package at the three houses.

Taghazout and Paradise Valley each deserve a guide of their own. We haven't published either yet.

## How do I get to Tamraght from Agadir airport?

Tamraght is about 40 km from Agadir Al Massira Airport (AGA), roughly 40 to 50 minutes by road depending on traffic. If you book a house through Surfer Element, the transfer is included both ways: a driver meets you when you land and takes you back for your flight home.

If you're arranging your own way, taxis, the airport bus and local buses all run to this coast, with different prices and speeds. The full comparison is in our [guide to getting from Agadir airport to Tamraght](/guide/agadir-airport-to-tamraght/).
